# Story / narrative / cinematic / realism

The scene is the star; UI is subtitles and a few quiet controls. `depth: flat` (cinematic, realism, milsim) or `soft` (friendly story games), `motion: calm` or `snappy`.

- **Cinematic menu**: full-bleed scene, a left-aligned column of text items with no button boxes; hover brightens the text and slides in a 2px accent bar. Title large and thin or large and heavy, never medium. 1px rules at 10-20% opacity. Corners 0-4.
- **Dialogue**: a box in the bottom third: dark translucent plate (or a bottom-up gradient scrim), speaker name on a small tab at the top-left in the accent colour, body text 26-30px with generous line height, a small continue arrow. Choices are a vertical list of rows, the hovered one marked.
- **Objectives**: one line top-centre or a short checklist top-left; fades after a few seconds.
- **Colour**: neutral lights and darks tinted to the scene's grade, one accent taken from the story's key colour.
- **Type**: one family used at extreme weights, or a serif title with a sans body. Sentence case for dialogue, tracked caps for labels.
- **Friendly story games** (camping, co-op adventures): rounded 12-16 plates, role cards with big art, vote cards in a row with a countdown bar; `depth: soft`.

**Make it this game's**: match the era and medium of the story (1940s noir = serif caps and cream on black; summer camp = hand-lettered labels on kraft paper; space drama = thin geometric sans). Let chapter cards and the dialogue name tab carry that identity.
