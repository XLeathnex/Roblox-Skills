# Horror / survival horror

The UI should almost not be there; every element that appears should feel like an object from the world. `depth: flat`, `motion: calm`.

- **Shape**: no panels. Text on the scene or on a dark scrim. Corners 0-3. Hairline rules at 60-90% transparency.
- **Colour**: tinted near-black, bone or paper off-white (never pure white), a dim mid-value for secondary text, and ONE accent (dried blood, sickly yellow, cold cyan) reserved for the selected item and danger.
- **Type**: the voice of an artefact from the setting: typewriter (case files, asylum), old serif (mansion, occult), condensed stencil (facility, military). Large quiet titles, small dim labels. No outlines; if text sits on the world, a soft dark text shadow.
- **Layout**: left-aligned, lots of empty dark. Menus are a vertical list of words; the selected one is brighter with a small accent mark.
- **HUD**: sparse and at the edges: a small item slot or two bottom-centre, objective text that fades in top-centre, a stamina bar as a thin line. Hide it when idle.
- **Atmosphere**: full-screen vignette, grain at 3-6%, optional spotlight; slow 0.4-1.2s fades, no bounce.
- **Mascot horror** (cartoon monsters) is the hybrid: chunky shapes but desaturated and dirty, hand-drawn marker type, thinner lips.

**Make it this game's**: decide what document or device the UI pretends to be (patient file, VHS camcorder overlay, ship's log, security terminal) and take labels, numbering and the accent from it. Creepster-style dripping fonts read as a costume; use them for a logo at most.
